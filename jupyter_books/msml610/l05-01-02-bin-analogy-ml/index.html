Cell 1: Visual Bin: Population of Marbles¶

Goal:

  • Visualize the concept of an unknown population of marbles in a bin
  • Introduce the parameter μ\muμ as the true (unknown) proportion of red marbles
  • Build intuition for how we have a fixed population with a fixed proportion

Plots:

  • Display a single plot:
    • Bin with Marbles: A 2D grid showing red and green marbles arranged in the bin
    • The visual distribution reflects the true proportion μ\muμ

Parameters:

  • mu (μ\muμ): True proportion of red marbles in the population (0 to 1)
  • seed: Random seed controlling the spatial arrangement of marbles (for reproducibility)

Key observations:

  • The bin represents a population with a fixed but “unknown” parameter μ\muμ
  • In real-world scenarios, we don’t know the true μ\muμ - we can only sample from the population
  • The spatial arrangement of marbles is random, but the overall proportion is always μ\muμ
  • This sets up the fundamental problem: How do we estimate μ\muμ from samples?

Cell 2.1: Limitations of Single Experiments¶

  • Single experiments don’t tell the full story:
    • We saw that ν\nuν can vary significantly across different random samples
    • One experiment gives us a point estimate, but no information about reliability
    • What we really need to know: P(∣ν−μ∣>ϵ)P(|\nu - \mu| > \epsilon)P(∣ν−μ∣>ϵ) - the probability that our estimate is “far” from truth
  • Critical questions to consider:
    • What if we got unlucky in our sample? How would we know?
    • How confident can we be that ν≈μ\nu \approx \muν≈μ based on a single observation?
    • Does sample size NNN matter? How much does it help?
    • Can we quantify the uncertainty in our estimate?
  • The solution: “Let’s repeat this many times...”
    • By running many experiments, we can:
      • Observe the distribution of ν\nuν values
      • Estimate P(∣ν−μ∣>ϵ)P(|\nu - \mu| > \epsilon)P(∣ν−μ∣>ϵ) empirically
      • Understand how sample size NNN affects the accuracy of ν\nuν

Cell 3: Monte Carlo Simulation: Distribution of ν\nuν¶

Goal:

  • Run many repeated sampling experiments to understand the distribution of ν\nuν
  • Empirically estimate P(∣ν−μ∣>ϵ)P(|\nu - \mu| > \epsilon)P(∣ν−μ∣>ϵ) - the probability that ν\nuν is far from μ\muμ
  • Observe how the distribution of ν\nuν concentrates around μ\muμ as we increase sample size NNN
  • Build intuition for the Law of Large Numbers and Central Limit Theorem

Plots:

  • Display two panels:
    • Distribution of nu: Histogram with KDE overlay showing the distribution of sample proportions across experiments
      • Green dashed line: True parameter μ\muμ
      • Red shaded regions: Areas where ∣ν−μ∣>ϵ|\nu - \mu| > \epsilon∣ν−μ∣>ϵ
      • Statistics box: Mean, standard deviation, and empirical probability
    • Key Insights: Text box summarizing parameters, results, and observations

Parameters:

  • mu (μ\muμ): True proportion of red marbles in the population (0 to 1)
  • N (NNN): Number of samples per experiment (sample size)

  • eps (ϵ\epsilonϵ): Tolerance threshold for defining “far from μ\muμ”

  • The distribution of ν\nuν values clusters around the true μ\muμ - demonstrating unbiasedness

  • The empirical probability P(∣ν−μ∣>ϵ)P(|\nu - \mu| > \epsilon)P(∣ν−μ∣>ϵ) decreases with larger NNN
  • The distribution approaches a normal shape - Central Limit Theorem

  • This connects to confidence intervals and the precision of statistical estimates

Cell 3.1: Observations from Monte Carlo Simulation¶

  • Distribution properties:
    • The distribution of ν\nuν values is centered around the true μ\muμ - this shows that ν\nuν is an unbiased estimator
    • The shape of the distribution approximates a normal (Gaussian) distribution - illustrating the Central Limit Theorem
    • Most ν\nuν values fall close to μ\muμ, with fewer extreme outliers
  • Effect of sample size NNN:
    • As NNN increases, the distribution becomes tighter (smaller variance: Var(ν)∝1N\text{Var}(\nu) \propto \frac{1}{N}Var(ν)∝N1​)
    • Larger NNN means more precise estimates - the “spread” of possible ν\nuν values shrinks
    • This is the Law of Large Numbers in action: ν→Pμ\nu \xrightarrow{P} \muνP​μ as N→∞N \to \inftyN→∞
  • Probabilistic guarantees:
    • The empirical probability P(∣ν−μ∣>ϵ)P(|\nu - \mu| > \epsilon)P(∣ν−μ∣>ϵ) decreases with larger NNN
    • We can quantify confidence: “With sample size NNN, approximately X% of experiments yield ν\nuν within ϵ\epsilonϵ of μ\muμ”
    • This connects to Hoeffding’s inequality and other concentration bounds
  • Connection to machine learning:
    • Replace “marbles in bin” with “data points” and “μ\muμ” with “expected error”
    • Sample proportion ν\nuν represents training error on NNN samples
    • True μ\muμ represents generalization error on the full population
    • Key insight: With enough samples (NNN large), training error ν\nuν is close to generalization error μ\muμ
