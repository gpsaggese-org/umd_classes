==== Why Study LLM Reasoning?
<why-study-llm-reasoning>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Motivation]: an agent is only as
  reliable as the #emph[reasoning] that drives its control loop
  - As we saw, the #text(fill: blue)[`Thought`] step is what chooses
    each action
  - A weak reasoner produces wasted actions, wrong plans, and
    unrecoverable errors
- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Question]: we have seen that models
  #emph[can] reason step by step, but
  - #emph[When] does that reasoning actually help?
  - #emph[How robust] is it to irrelevant changes in the prompt?
  - #emph[Can] a model check and fix its own reasoning?
- This lesson looks #emph[inside] the reasoning step

==== What Do We Mean by "Reasoning"?
<what-do-we-mean-by-reasoning>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Definition]: #emph[reasoning] is
  producing a conclusion through a chain of intermediate inferences,
  each following from the previous ones
  - Contrast with #emph[recall] (the answer is retrieved in a single
    step)
- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Example]:
  - Recall: #emph["What is the capital of France?"] $arrow.r$ direct
    lookup
  - Reasoning: #emph["If a train leaves at 2pm going 60mph…"] $arrow.r$
    multiple dependent steps
- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Key idea]: reasoning needs
  #emph[serial depth];, several dependent steps that cannot be collapsed
  into one
  - For an LLM, those intermediate steps are made of #emph[tokens];, so
    reasoning is literally text the model writes and then reads back

==== Reasoning as Serial Computation: Recap and Beyond
<reasoning-as-serial-computation-recap-and-beyond>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Recap]: a single forward pass is a
  #emph[fixed-depth] circuit
  - It cannot carry out an arbitrarily long chain of dependent steps in
    one shot
  - Writing tokens adds serial depth like further forward passes
- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Beyond the recap]: this raises four
  questions
  + Are there #emph[variants] of chain-of-thought that reason better?
  + Does reasoning #emph[require] a special prompt, or is it latent in
    the model?
  + Why is reasoning #emph[brittle] to superficial changes like premise
    order?
  + Can a model #emph[self-correct] its own reasoning?
- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Key idea]: #emph[capability] and
  #emph[brittleness] are two sides of the same mechanism

==== Chain-of-Thought: Quick Recap
<chain-of-thought-quick-recap>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Recap] #emph[Chain-of-thought] (CoT)
  prompting (Wei et al., 2022) makes the model emit intermediate steps
  before the answer

  ```
  prompt> Let's think step by step
  ```

- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Key idea]: CoT trades extra tokens
  (time) for extra reasoning depth

  - Externalizes reasoning so it can be inspected and continued

- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Question]: CoT is one recipe, but a
  whole family grew around it

  - What do the main variants change, and why?

==== Why Does CoT Help? Two Hypotheses
<why-does-cot-help-two-hypotheses>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Question]: is CoT giving the model
  #emph[more computation];, or just a better #emph[prompt pattern];?

  - #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Computation]: each step is a
    forward pass, so CoT adds serial depth the architecture lacks

  - #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Elicitation]: the steps put the
    model into a region of its distribution where the right facts and
    format are more likely

- #text(fill: rgb("#8B00FF"), weight: "bold")[Key idea]: both are partly
  true

  - Computation view: helps most on #emph[serial] tasks (e.g.,
    arithmetic, multi-hop)
  - Elicitation view: reasoning may be #emph[latent] and recoverable
    without a prompt

==== Zero-Shot vs.~Few-Shot CoT
<zero-shot-vs.-few-shot-cot>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Definition]: #emph[zero-shot CoT]
  adds only a trigger phrase (#emph["Let's think step by step"];) with
  no worked examples

- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Definition]: #emph[few-shot CoT]
  places a handful of worked examples (with their reasoning) in the
  prompt before the target question

  - Demonstrates both the format #emph[and] the reasoning style

#styled-table(
  headers: ("Variant", "Prompt cost", "When to prefer"),
  rows: (
    ("Zero-shot CoT", "Low (one phrase)", "General reasoning, cheap"),
    ("Few-shot CoT", "High (examples)", "Format matters, niche task"),
  ),
)
- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Key idea]: both rely on an explicit
  textual trigger to #emph[switch on] reasoning, an assumption we
  question in the next section

==== Self-Consistency
<self-consistency>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Problem]: a single CoT chain can take
  one wrong turn and commit to it

- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Solution]: #emph[self-consistency]
  (Wang et al., 2022)

  + Sample #emph[many] reasoning chains with temperature $> 0$
  + Extract the final answer from each
  + Return the #emph[majority] answer (marginalize over reasoning paths)
    $ hat(y) = arg sans("max")_y #h(0em) sum_i bb(1) \[ upright("answer") \( upright("chain")_i \) = y \] $

- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Intuition]:

  - Reasoning becomes a #emph[distribution];, not a single trace to
    trust
  - Many #emph[different] correct derivations reach the same answer
  - Wrong derivations scatter across many different answers

==== Search Over Reasoning: Tree and Graph of Thought
<search-over-reasoning-tree-and-graph-of-thought>

  - #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Problem]: self-consistency samples
    #emph[independent] chains and cannot revisit a promising partial path
  
  - #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Solution]: treat reasoning as
    #emph[search] over a structure of partial thoughts
  
    - #emph[Tree-of-Thought];: branch into candidate steps, evaluate,

    - #emph[Graph-of-Thought];: allow merging and reuse of intermediate

==== Problem: Does Reasoning Require a Prompt?
<problem-does-reasoning-require-a-prompt>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Question]:

  - Every variant so far #emph[asks] the model to reason (through a
    phrase or examples)
  - Does reasoning even need an explicit CoT #emph[prompt];?
  - Is step-by-step reasoning a capability #emph[created] by the prompt,
    or one that is #emph[latent] and merely #emph[uncovered] by it?

- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Motivation]: if reasoning is
  latent, prompt engineering is only #emph[one] way to access it, and
  maybe not the best one

- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Key idea] (Wang & Zhou, 2024): the
  way we #emph[decode];, not the prompt, can be what hides the model's
  reasoning

==== Greedy Decoding Hides Reasoning
<greedy-decoding-hides-reasoning>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Observation]: standard #emph[greedy]
  decoding always takes the single most-likely first token
  - For a math question, that token is often the final answer
- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Problem]: committing to that first
  token can skip the reasoning entirely
  - The model #emph[has] a reasoning path, but greedy decoding never
    enters it
- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Key idea]: the first token is a
  #emph[fork in the road]
  - One branch answers immediately (often wrong)
  - Another branch starts a chain of thought

==== CoT-Decoding: The Algorithm
<cot-decoding-the-algorithm>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Input]: a question with a plain
  prompt (no #emph["think step by step"];)

- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Algorithm] (#emph[CoT-decoding];):

  + At the #emph[first] decoding step, take the #emph[top-$k$] candidate
    tokens (not just the top-1)
  + Continue greedy decoding from each of the $k$ alternatives
  + This yields $k$ candidate completions, some containing a CoT
  + Score each path by its answer confidence and pick the best

- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Key idea]: branching only at the
  first step is enough to #emph[surface] latent reasoning paths already
  inside a pretrained model

==== The Confidence Signal
<the-confidence-signal>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Problem]: how to pick the reasoning
  path among the $k$ without knowing the answer?

- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Solution]: measure the model's
  #emph[confidence] in its final answer tokens

  - Paths that contain a chain of thought show a #emph[larger
    probability gap] between the top and second answer token
    $ Delta_(upright("answer")) = Pr \( upright("token")_1 \) - Pr \( upright("token")_2 \) $

- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Interpretation]: when the model has
  #emph[reasoned];, it is more #emph[committed] to its answer, and
  $Delta_(upright("answer"))$ is high

  - The presence of CoT #emph[correlates] with answer confidence, giving
    an unsupervised way to select the reasoning path

==== CoT-Decoding: Findings and Implications
<cot-decoding-findings-and-implications>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Findings] (Wang & Zhou, 2024):
  - Reasoning paths are #emph[frequently] present among top-$k$
    alternatives, even with no CoT prompt
  - Selecting them by confidence recovers much of the accuracy of
    prompted CoT
  - Instruction tuning makes CoT easier to elicit but does not
    #emph[create] the ability
- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Interpretation]: reasoning is
  (partly) an #emph[intrinsic] property of the pretrained model,
  obscured by greedy decoding
  - Yet this latent reasoning is fragile

==== Premise Order Matters: The Finding
<premise-order-matters-the-finding>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Question]: does an LLM respect this
  invariance, or does merely #emph[reordering] the same facts change its
  answer?
  - Robustness to irrelevant changes is a basic test of whether a model
    #emph[reasons] or #emph[pattern-matches]
- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Fact]: in formal logic, the order
  of premises is irrelevant
  - ${ A \, B \, C } tack.r Q$ holds regardless of how $A \, B \, C$ are
    listed
- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Answer] (Chen et al., 2024): LLMs
  are surprisingly #emph[sensitive] to premise order
  - Reasoning is not fully logical
- #text(fill: rgb("#8B00FF"), weight: "bold")[Findings]:
  - Take a reasoning problem and permute only the #emph[order] of its
    premises, keeping the content identical
  - Performance is #emph[best] when premise order matches the order
    needed in the reasoning steps (the ground-truth proof order)
  - Reordering causes a performance drop of #emph[over 30%] on deductive
    tasks
  - The model prefers to consume premises in the order it will
    #emph[use] them, rather than searching for the relevant one
  - A logically irrelevant change causes a large, systematic accuracy
    loss

==== Worked Example: Reordering Premises
<worked-example-reordering-premises>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Example]: same facts, two orders

#styled-table(
  headers: ("Forward order (easy)", "Shuffled order (hard)"),
  rows: (
    ("If A then B", "If C then D"),
    ("If B then C", "If A then B"),
    ("If C then D", "If B then C"),
    ("Given A, prove D", "Given A, prove D"),
  ),
)
- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Interpretation]: the left column
  lets the model chain $A arrow.r B arrow.r C arrow.r D$ by reading top
  to bottom
  - The right column forces it to #emph[jump around] to find the next
    usable premise
- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Key idea]: extra "distance" between
  where a premise sits and where it is needed degrades reasoning

==== Measuring the Effect on Math (R-GSM)
<measuring-the-effect-on-math-r-gsm>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Problem]: is premise-order
  sensitivity limited to synthetic logic puzzles?

- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Solution]: #emph[R-GSM] (Chen et
  al., 2024), a #emph[reordered] variant of the GSM8K grade-school math
  benchmark

  - Same problems, sentences reordered without changing the math

- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Finding]: accuracy drops
  #emph[significantly] on R-GSM versus GSM8K across models

  - The effect grows with the number of premises to reorder
  - Brittleness to order shows up on #emph[realistic] math word
    problems, not just toy logic

==== Why Order Matters: An Autoregressive Bias
<why-order-matters-an-autoregressive-bias>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Question]: why should a left-to-right
  model care about premise order?

- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Interpretation]:

  - As we know, attention and generation are "causal" (i.e., the model
    builds its chain forward)
  - It is easier to attend to the #emph[next] needed premise if it is
    #emph[nearby and ahead];, not buried earlier or later
  - Out-of-order premises force long-range, non-monotonic lookups the
    model does poorly
  - The same causal mechanism that #emph[enables] serial reasoning also
    #emph[biases] it toward a particular input order

==== Brittleness Beyond Order
<brittleness-beyond-order>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Remark]: premise order is one of
  several #emph[superficial] perturbations that break reasoning
  - Irrelevant #emph[distractor] sentences added to a problem
  - Renaming entities or changing surface wording
  - Changing numeric values that should not affect the method
- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Key idea]:
  - A truly reasoning system would be invariant to all of these
  - Current LLMs are only #emph[approximately] so
  - Robustness, not raw accuracy, is the frontier for reasoning

==== The Depth Limit and CoT
<the-depth-limit-and-cot>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Recap]:
  - A constant-depth transformer is a highly #emph[parallel];,
    fixed-depth circuit
  - Without intermediate tokens, its per-token power is bounded
  - It struggles with problems that are #emph[inherently serial]
- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Question]: can we make this precise?
  - #emph[Exactly] how much does chain-of-thought add to what a
    transformer can compute?

==== CoT Empowers Serial Computation: The Result
<cot-empowers-serial-computation-the-result>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Theorem] (informal, Li et al., 2024):
  consider constant-depth transformers with constant-bit precision
  - #emph[Without] CoT: they can only solve problems in $sans(A C)^0$ (a
    strict subset of $sans(T C)^0$), i.e., highly parallel problems
  - #emph[With] $T$ steps of CoT: they can solve #emph[any] problem
    computable by a Boolean circuit of size $T$
- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Interpretation]:
  - Adding $T$ reasoning tokens is like being handed a circuit with $T$
    gates of #emph[serial] compute
  - CoT does not just help empirically, it #emph[provably] enlarges the
    class of solvable problems

==== Intuition: Each Token Simulates One Gate
<intuition-each-token-simulates-one-gate>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Intuition]: the model uses generated
  tokens as a #emph[scratchpad] (a tape)
  + Each CoT step writes one intermediate result as tokens
  + Later steps #emph[attend] to earlier results and combine them
  + $T$ steps simulate a size-$T$ computation, one gate at a time
- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Example]: tasks that are hard in
  one pass but easy with CoT
  - Composition of permutations
  - Iterated squaring / modular arithmetic
  - Evaluating a Boolean circuit step by step
- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Fact]: #emph[Depth in time] (tokens)
  supplies the extra power

==== Trading Tokens for Depth
<trading-tokens-for-depth>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Interpretation]: there is a
  #emph[depth-for-tokens] trade-off
  - A shallow model with a long CoT can match tasks that would otherwise
    need a much deeper network
- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Consequences]:
  - Serial tasks #emph[require] enough CoT tokens; truncating the
    scratchpad caps capability
  - "Answer immediately" prompts throw away exactly the resource serial
    problems need
  - Longer allowed reasoning (bigger token budget) is a genuine
    #emph[capability] lever, not just style
- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Key idea]: reasoning length is
  compute
  - But more compute does not imply the model can #emph[judge] its own
    output, our last theme

==== Can Models Fix Their Own Mistakes?
<can-models-fix-their-own-mistakes>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Motivation]: a natural idea for
  reliability is to let the model #emph[review and revise] its own
  answer

  - #emph["Here is your answer; find any mistakes and correct them"]

- #text(fill: orange, weight: "bold")[Definition]: #emph[intrinsic
  self-correction] = the model critiques and revises using #emph[only]
  its own capabilities, with no oracle label or tool

- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Question]: does this
  #emph[intrinsic self-correction] (no external feedback, no ground
  truth) actually improve reasoning?

- #text(fill: teal, weight: "bold")[Finding] (Huang et al., 2023): with
  no external feedback, self-correction #emph[does not] reliably improve
  reasoning

  - At times, performance #emph[degrades] after the model "corrects"
    itself

- #text(fill: rgb("#00FFFF"), weight: "bold")[Problem]: earlier
  optimistic results often used #emph[oracle labels]

  - The loop was stopped only when the answer became correct
  - This #emph[leaks] the ground truth and hides the true (weak)
    behavior
  - Remove the oracle, and the gains largely disappear
  - The model cannot tell #emph[which] of its answers was right

- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Remark]: #emph[multi-agent debate]
  and re-prompting mostly recover the benefit of #emph[self-consistency]
  (sampling and voting), not a new correcting ability

- #text(fill: rgb("#8B00FF"), weight: "bold")[Key idea]: verification,
  not just generation, is the missing ingredient

==== When Self-Correction Does Help
<when-self-correction-does-help>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Solution]: self-correction works when
  feedback is #emph[external] and #emph[reliable]

  - A #emph[code interpreter] or unit tests (does it run and pass?)
  - A #emph[search / retrieval] tool that supplies missing facts
  - A separate #emph[verifier] trained to judge answers

- #text(fill: green, weight: "bold")[Key idea]: the fix is to
  #emph[ground] the correction signal, exactly the grounding lesson from
  ReAct (Lesson 16.3)

- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Remark]: how to #emph[build] these
  signals?

  - Inference-time techniques (self-debugging, verifier-guided search)
  - Learning to reason from preference and verifiable rewards

==== LLM Reasoning: Summary and Open Questions
<llm-reasoning-summary-and-open-questions>
- #text(fill: red, weight: "bold")[Key takeaways]:
  - Reasoning is #emph[serial computation] made of tokens; CoT and its
    variants (self-consistency, tree search) trade compute for
    reliability
  - Reasoning is partly #emph[latent];: #emph[CoT-decoding] surfaces it
    from top-$k$ first tokens with no prompt, selected by answer
    confidence
  - Reasoning is #emph[brittle];: reordering logically identical
    premises drops accuracy over 30% (R-GSM), a sign of
    pattern-matching, not pure logic
  - CoT #emph[provably] adds power: $T$ reasoning steps let a
    constant-depth transformer simulate a size-$T$ circuit (beyond
    $sans(A C)^0$)
  - Models #emph[cannot] yet self-correct reasoning intrinsically;
    reliable correction needs an #emph[external] verifier
- #text(fill: blue, weight: "bold")[Open questions]:
  - How do we make reasoning #emph[robust] to superficial perturbations?
  - How do we build reliable verifiers so agents can safely
    self-correct?
  - How do memory and planning extend single-shot reasoning across a
    full loop?
